Items in a Series

Use commas to separate three or more items in a list. Always use a comma before "and" or "or" at the end (called the Oxford comma) to make your writing clear!
✓ I packed my backpack with snacks, games, and my favorite book.
✓ We can watch a movie, play video games, or build a fort.
✓ My puppy is fluffy, playful, and super friendly.

Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

Use a comma before coordinating conjunctions (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) when they connect two complete sentences (independent clauses).
✓ I wanted to go to the pool, but it was raining outside.
✓ She practiced her spelling words, so she aced the test.
✓ We can ride our bikes, or we can go skateboarding.
✗ I like pizza and tacos. (No comma - "tacos" isn't a complete sentence)

Equal Adjectives

Use a comma between two adjectives that equally describe a noun. Test: If you can put "and" between them or reverse their order and it still makes sense, use a comma!
✓ She has a kind, helpful teacher. (kind AND helpful)
✓ It was a long, boring movie. (long AND boring)
✗ I have three blue marbles. (Can't say "three AND blue")

Dates

When writing full dates, use commas to separate the day from the year, and put a comma after the year if the sentence continues. For just month and year, no comma is needed!
✓ My birthday is June 15, 2015.
✓ On December 25, 2024, we had a huge snowball fight.
✓ I was born in May 2015. (No comma - just month and year)

Addresses

Use commas to separate parts of an address: between the city and state, and after the state if the sentence continues. Don't put a comma between the state and ZIP code!
✓ I live in Orlando, Florida.
✓ She moved to Austin, Texas, last summer.
✓ I visited Paris, France, with my family.

Introductory Phrases and Clauses

Use a comma after an introductory word, phrase, or clause that comes before the main part of the sentence. This helps readers know where the introduction ends!
✓ After the game, we went out for ice cream.
✓ Yes, I would love to come to your sleepover!
✓ When it started raining, we ran inside quickly.

Explanatory and Appositive Phrases

An appositive is a word or phrase that renames or explains a noun. Use commas before and after an appositive phrase to set it off from the rest of the sentence.
✓ My best friend, Sarah, loves to read mystery books.
✓ Mr. Johnson, our science teacher, is really funny.
✓ My dog, a golden retriever, loves to swim.

Interruptions and Interjections

Use commas to set off words or phrases that interrupt the flow of a sentence, or interjections (words that show emotion). These commas help readers know what's the main idea and what's extra information.
✓ The answer, I think, is forty-two.
✓ Wow, that roller coaster was incredible!
✓ This homework, believe it or not, is actually fun.
